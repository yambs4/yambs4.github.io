Telling the inflation story in Ethiopia

Inflation is more than a headline rate. It is a story about prices, purchasing power, expectations, and the different ways households experience economic change.

This manuscript looks at how the inflation experience in Ethiopia can be explained clearly and responsibly. It brings together the measured movement of prices with the questions that matter to households, firms, and policymakers: what is becoming more expensive, who is most exposed, and how should the story be communicated?

The aim is not to reduce a complex macroeconomic episode to a single number. A useful inflation story should make the data visible while keeping distribution, timing, and lived experience in view.
